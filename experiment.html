When does a piano note end?

About two and a half seconds into the Rachmaninoff example, the pianist plays a C above middle C. TorchPitch finds the right key and starts it within 32 milliseconds of the reference. Then it lets go. The MIDI from the performance holds that C for about eight tenths of a second. The model gives it less than two tenths.

To see what a small model could learn from piano alone, I adapted Spotify’s Basic Pitch architecture and trained it on MAESTRO, a collection of piano recordings paired with the MIDI captured during each performance. I trained once on 50 recordings, then again on 200. I tested both runs on the same 160 two-second excerpts from ten recordings held out of training.

The larger run picked up 81% of the reference notes, up from 66%. It still added extra notes. Counting both misses and extras, its F1 score for pitch and onset rose from 62% to 69%. Requiring the endings to match as well brought that score down to 32%.

Try the passage twice. Listen to the original first, then play the transcription and follow the bars in the piano roll. Each bar starts where the model hears a key strike. Its length is the model’s guess about how long to hold it.
